A short history of taekwondo

How a group of post-war Korean martial-art schools became one of the world’s most widely practiced martial arts and an Olympic and Paralympic sport, and where the forms on this site come from.

How modern taekwondo took shape

Roots. Kukkiwon’s history connects taekwondo with older Korean empty-hand arts such as Subak and the folk art Taekkyeon, and notes that judo, kendo and other foreign styles reached Korea under Japanese rule.

The kwans, 1946–1960. After liberation, those imported arts were taught in new schools, the Five Kwans. By Kukkiwon’s account, they added kicks drawn from Taekkyeon, and after the Korean War the name “Taekwondo” took hold as the schools moved toward unity.

One association, 1961–1970. In the early 1960s a national association brought the schools together under shared dan tests and poomsae. Sparring entered Korea’s National Sports Festival in 1963, and the art spread abroad, to Asia, Europe and the United States.

Two kinds of organization. Taekwondo today is shaped by bodies with different jobs. Kukkiwon, founded in 1972 and known as the World Taekwondo Headquarters, leads taekwondo as a martial art and cultural tradition, including the poomsae taught in WT/Kukkiwon schools. World Taekwondo, founded in 1973, governs it as an international sport, including Olympic competition. Alongside them, the International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF), founded in 1966, teaches its own patterns and runs its own competitions.

Source: Kukkiwon, History of Taekwondo; founding dates as cited in the timeline below.

Timeline

  1. 1946–1960

    Early modern taekwondo: the post-war kwans. Kukkiwon’s history treats these years, when the Five Kwans emerged, as taekwondo’s early period.

    Source: Kukkiwon, History of Taekwondo (Early Taekwondo, 1946–1960)

  2. 11 April 1955

    The ITF dates the name “taekwondo” to this day. Kukkiwon’s history places the name after the Korean War, without a date.

    Source: ITF, Our history

  3. 1963

    Sparring becomes an official event at Korea’s 44th National Sports Festival.

    Source: Kukkiwon, History of Taekwondo

  4. 22 March 1966

    The International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF) is founded. The ITF names General Choi Hong Hi as its founder. The ITF patterns on this site, such as Chon-Ji and Dan-Gun, come from this tradition.

    Source: ITF, Our history

  5. 30 November 1972

    Kukkiwon is founded. Kukkiwon is the source of the Taegeuk and black-belt poomsae taught in WT/Kukkiwon schools, including the forms in this site’s WT/Kukkiwon lessons.

    Source: Kukkiwon, Introduction of Kukkiwon

  6. 28 May 1973

    World Taekwondo is founded. World Taekwondo is the international federation for Olympic taekwondo competition.

    Source: World Taekwondo, Annual Report October 2021–July 2022

  7. 1974

    The ITF holds its first World Championships, in Montreal.

    Source: ITF, Our history

  8. 1988 and 1992

    Taekwondo is a demonstration sport at the Seoul 1988 and Barcelona 1992 Olympic Games. Demonstration events did not award Olympic medals.

    Source: IOC Olympic Studies Centre, Taekwondo: history at the Olympic Games

  9. 1994

    The International Olympic Committee decides to add taekwondo to the Olympic program.

    Source: IOC Olympic Studies Centre, Taekwondo: history at the Olympic Games

  10. 2000

    Taekwondo is first contested for Olympic medals, at Sydney 2000.

    Source: IOC Olympic Studies Centre, Taekwondo: history at the Olympic Games

  11. 2 September 2021

    Para taekwondo makes its Paralympic debut, at the Tokyo 2020 Paralympic Games (held in 2021).

    Source: International Paralympic Committee; see also Para taekwondo overview

The forms on TKDflow

The lessons here follow three traditions: WT/Kukkiwon poomsae such as the eight Taegeuk forms, Koryo and Keumgang; ITF patterns such as Chon-Ji, Dan-Gun and Do-San; and the ATA’s Songahm forms. Schools within each tradition can still differ in details, so your instructor’s version comes first.
