Three ways in

Choose your path

I’m a new student

  1. Ask your instructor which style your school teaches and the name of the form you are learning now. How styles differ
  2. Find that form in the library and open its lesson.
  3. Watch it once at full speed. Then play it at 0.25×, or pause and step a frame or a pose at a time. To follow along facing the same way, move the camera behind the performer.
  4. Save the form so you can pick up at your last pose next time. How saving works

I’m a parent or guardian

  1. Find out which style and which form your child is working on. Belt colors do not tell you this on their own. Styles and belts
  2. Watch the lesson together at a slower speed and use pose links to return to the part that is giving trouble. Lesson tools
  3. No account is needed to study. Accounts are for ages 13 and over. Ages and accounts
  4. Thinking about a competition? Read the first tournament guide with your child’s instructor.

I’m coming back to training

  1. Check with your school whether its curriculum or form names have changed since you last trained.
  2. Use the library’s style and belt filters with Curriculum order to see what comes next. Open the library
  3. Use pose links and the keyboard to jump to the sections you have forgotten. Lesson tools
  4. Read lesson answers for common questions about poses.

Curriculum differences

Styles and schools: WT/Kukkiwon, ITF and ATA

Taekwondo schools follow different organizations, and each has its own forms, names and counting. Your school’s curriculum decides which form you learn next. The library groups its forms into three styles.

WT / Kukkiwon poomsae

Kukkiwon in Seoul describes itself as the World Taekwondo Headquarters. Its forms are called poomsae. Kukkiwon’s poomsae pages list Taegeuk 1 to 8 and the black-belt forms from Koryo to Ilyeo, and place the Taegeuk series before Koryo, Keumgang and Taebaek. World Taekwondo, the sport federation, publishes its own rules for poomsae competition.

14 lessons on TKDflow, from Taegeuk Il Jang to Jitae. Browse WT/Kukkiwon forms

ITF patterns (tul)

ITF-style schools call their forms tul, or patterns. Details such as how movements are counted can vary, so follow the named reference your school uses.

6 lessons on TKDflow, from Chon-Ji to Joong-Gun. Each lesson names the booklet or school curriculum it follows. Browse ITF patterns

ATA Songahm forms

The American Taekwondo Association (ATA Martial Arts) teaches Songahm taekwondo. Its official Songahm #1 sheet numbers 18 moves between the ready position and Bah Ro, and gives directions as compass points on a diagram. ATA is a different organization from American Taekwondo United (ATU), which appears in the tournament guide.

3 lessons on TKDflow: Songahm 1, 2 and 3. Browse ATA forms

Belt colors do not line up between styles, or even between schools in one style. A yellow belt at one school may be learning a different form from a yellow belt elsewhere. The library’s belt labels show a typical placement, not a rule.

For how these organizations came about, read the short history of taekwondo. To see each style performed, open the video gallery.

Choose a form

Find your form

  1. Ask for the form’s name. Your instructor’s name for it is the most reliable starting point.
  2. Search by name. Spellings vary: Taegeuk Il Jang is also written Taegeuk 1, Sipjin is also Shipjin, Chon-Ji is also Cheon-Ji, and Songahm 1 is also Songahm #1. The library search accepts these.
  3. No name yet? Filter by style and belt, then sort by Curriculum order. Treat the result as a suggestion to check with your school.
  4. Keep your school’s order. My school’s forms lets you write down your school’s own belt or level names and the forms for each, then print the list. It is saved only in your browser.
  5. Know a technique but not the form? Find a technique lists every lesson pose that uses it.
  6. Choosing between two forms? Compare two forms shows their style, series, typical belt or level and lesson counts side by side.

Reading a lesson

Animation poses, official counts and directions

Poses are not the official count

Each lesson splits the form into study poses. Pose numbers include the ready and return positions, and one counted movement can become two poses: a kick and the punch that follows it, for example. The form’s official count is shown separately in the lesson details. Taegeuk Il Jang is commonly counted as 18 movements; its animation has 20 poses.

Left and right are the performer’s

“Left” and “right” always mean the performer’s own left and right, never the sides of your screen. You can move the camera to any side, and it stays where you put it. At the ready position, a camera behind the performer shows the form facing the way you would; from the front, their left appears on your right. After a turn, keep using the performer’s own left and right: the camera does not mirror or follow the way they face.

Your school or an organization’s form sheet may describe directions differently, for example as compass points or lines on a diagram. When they disagree with the animation, follow your instructor.

Hearing Korean words such as Chagi or Makgi? Taekwondo words explained gives short meanings, with lesson examples where there are some.

Using a lesson

Lesson tools

Play, pause and step
Play the whole form, or step forward and back one frame or one pose at a time. With the player selected, use ← and → to step a frame, Shift with ← or → to step a pose, Home and End to jump to the first and last pose, Space or K to play and pause, and R to replay the current move once. Replay move plays the current move once, with its voice and effects, then pauses on its pose.
Speed
Choose 0.25×, 0.5× or 1× for playback. Slow playback helps you see foot placement and the path of a block; to stop on a detail, pause and step instead.
Camera
Drag the 3D view to move the camera around the performer, or use the camera buttons: move left or right (each press goes to the next side), raise or lower, zoom out or in, and Reset. Whole body keeps every limb in frame. Armwork moves in on the head and arms. Footwork follows both knees and feet: it moves in for stances and steps and eases back out before a kick, so the kicking foot stays in view. With the player selected, use A and D to move around (Shift for the next side), W and S to raise or lower, + and - to zoom, 0 to reset, and 1, 2 or 3 for Whole body, Armwork or Footwork. On a phone, drag sideways and pinch to zoom; vertical swipes still scroll the page, and in Study view you can drag in any direction. The camera stays where you put it when the performer turns.
Voice and effects
Spoken move instructions and short sound effects such as the kihap follow the animation. Switch either off with its button.
Study view
Enlarges the player and hides the rest of the page while you practice. Press Escape or select Exit study view to return.
Pose links
Copy a link to the pose on screen to share it or come back to it later.
Find a technique
Search every lesson for a block, strike or kick and open the lesson paused on each pose that uses it. Find a technique
Read and print
Every lesson also has a plain-text companion listing each pose with its count, turn, kihap and spoken instruction, for reading on a phone or printing for class. Read and print companions
All poses
A written list of every pose with Korean terms where the lesson has them. It stays available if the 3D view cannot load.

Saved learning and help

Save your place and ask questions

  • Without an account: Save form and your last pose are kept on this browser only. Anyone using the same browser can see them.
  • With an account: once your email is confirmed and your profile is complete, saved forms and progress are kept privately with your account and follow you across devices. You can copy this browser’s saves into your account from My learning.
  • Practiced is your own reminder. It is not a belt award or an assessment.
  • Lesson answers are public replies from TKDflow community moderators to common questions. Read lesson answers
  • Help with this lesson, on each lesson page, lets members aged 13 and over with a confirmed account and completed profile ask moderators a private question about a pose.

Words you will hear

Glossary

Poomsae
Forms in WT/Kukkiwon taekwondo.
Tul
Patterns in ITF taekwon-do.
Form
A set sequence of techniques performed as a pattern. ATA and many schools use this word.
Junbi
Ready: the position before the first movement.
Baro (Bah Ro)
Return: the call to come back to the ready position at the end.
Kihap
A short shout on a set movement.
Gup / geup
A color-belt grade. Many schools count down toward black belt.
Dan
A black-belt degree.
Study pose
One step of a TKDflow animation. Not the same as an official movement count.
Performer’s left
The animated character’s own left side, whichever way they face and wherever the camera is.
