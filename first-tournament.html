Your first taekwondo tournament

A practical guide for students and families preparing for a first USA Taekwondo, AAU Taekwondo or American Taekwondo United event, with step-by-step checklists for forms and sparring.

The event’s official packet always comes first. Rules, divisions, fees, deadlines and equipment differ between organizations, events and years. This guide explains what to check; it is not an organizer’s rulebook and does not confirm anyone’s eligibility. Sources were checked on 17 November 2026; later changes are not reflected.

How tournaments differ

Ask your instructor whether a local or open event suits your first tournament.

Local and open events
Run by a school, club or regional group, sometimes sanctioned by a national organization. Some require a membership; others do not.
State, regional and qualifying events
Some organizations use these to qualify athletes for later championships. Whether an event qualifies you for anything, and for what, depends on that organization’s rules for that year.
National championships
Usually have stricter entry, documentation and qualification rules, set for each championship.

Memberships, rankings and qualifications from one organization do not carry over to another organization’s events unless that organizer says they do.

Organizations

Which organization runs your event?

Check the event page or packet for the organizer or sanctioning body. Three organizations covered here are often confused with each other and with others.

USA Taekwondo USAT · USATKD

The U.S. national governing body for Olympic-style taekwondo. Its events use its published competition rules.

  • Athletes register for events from their member profile in USA Taekwondo’s Sport:80 system, and the guide lists belt details the profile needs (athlete registration guide).
  • Athletes aged 18 or over need completed SafeSport training that stays valid through the event dates (same guide).
  • If an event is not in the list of events you can register for, the guide says to ask USA Taekwondo’s events team.
  • Coaches register separately and may need a course for the specific discipline through USA Taekwondo University (coach registration guide).
  • Some national championships ask for extra documents. These apply only to the events that state them.

USA Taekwondo competition rules · USA Taekwondo events on TKDflow

AAU Taekwondo Amateur Athletic Union

The taekwondo program of the Amateur Athletic Union, a multi-sport organization with its own membership and events.

  • AAU’s 2025–26 handbook treats traditional forms (patterns) and sport poomsae as separate events, and lists point, continuous-point and Olympic-style sparring (AAU Taekwondo handbook, updated 27 February 2026). It also describes district and regional events that lead to national events; check the current edition and your event for the route that applies.
  • Example from one event: the organizer of a 2027 Arizona AAU qualifier states that a current AAU membership is required, that membership may not be included in the entry fee, and that coaches need a current online coach certification and an event coaching pass (Arizona AAU tournament information).
  • AAU offers online taekwondo certification; 2027 online certification was available when checked (AAU certification).
  • AAU lists upcoming national taekwondo events on its future dates page. Exact 2027 dates and locations were still to be announced when checked.

AAU Taekwondo · AAU events on TKDflow

American Taekwondo United ATU

A U.S. taekwondo organization with its own membership and tournaments. It is not the ATA (American Taekwondo Association / ATA Martial Arts) and not the Asian Taekwondo Union, which also uses the initials ATU.

  • ATU’s original site points to its current site, joinatu.com, whose event information and registration page is the place to start. Spectator tickets are sold separately at joinatu.online; a spectator ticket is not an athlete entry or a coach pass.
  • Example from one event: ATU’s 2026 Nationals information packet separated Grassroots traditional forms and sparring from World Class sport poomsae and sparring, listed separate coach and membership fees and division-specific weigh-in times, and required competitors to bring their own sparring safety equipment (2026 ATU Nationals packet).
  • No official 2027 ATU national championship announcement was found when checked on 16 November 2026.

Disciplines

Forms or sparring?

Forms

You perform a set pattern while judges score it under the event’s rules. Some organizations run traditional forms (patterns from your style) and sport poomsae (a separate WT-style ruleset) as different events, so check which one your division is. Divisions are usually grouped by age and rank, and the allowed forms can depend on your division and round.

Study forms in the TKDflow library

Sparring

You compete in scored matches against an opponent under the event’s rules, wearing the protective equipment the event requires. Formats differ: some organizations offer point, continuous-point and Olympic-style sparring as separate events. Divisions can also depend on weight and experience, and there may be a weigh-in. Scoring, contact rules and equipment differ by organization, format and division.

Many first-time competitors enter one discipline. Ask your instructor which one suits you.

Ask

Questions to ask

Your coach or instructor

  • Is this event a good first step for me?
  • Which division and forms should I enter?
  • Which equipment do I need, and is any of it provided?
  • Will you be coaching at the event, and do you need a coach registration?
  • What should I practise before the event?

The event organizer

  • Which membership, if any, is required, and by when?
  • How is age counted for divisions?
  • What is the refund or division-change policy?
  • When are check-in and any weigh-in, and what should I bring?
  • What are the rules for spectators and for guardians of minors?

Words you will hear

Glossary

Event packet
The organizer’s official information for one event: rules, divisions, schedule, fees and policies.
Sanctioned event
An event approved by an organization under its rules. A sanction does not make it count for another organization.
Open tournament
An event open to competitors beyond one school or club. Entry rules still apply.
Qualifier
An event that can lead to entry at a later championship under that organization’s rules for that season.
Division
The group you compete in, usually set by age and rank, and for sparring often by weight.
Bracket
The order of matches or rounds in a division.
Ring or court
The marked competition area where a division takes place.
Ring call
The announcement telling a division or competitor to report to a ring.
Staging area
Where competitors wait before going to their ring. Events use different names for it.
Check-in
Confirming your arrival and registration on the day.
Weigh-in
Recording a sparring competitor’s weight for divisions that use weight.
Poomsae
Forms: set patterns of movements, performed and judged.
Kyorugi
Sparring: scored matches between two competitors.
