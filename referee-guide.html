Competitions

A practical guide to refereeing sparring

Who decides what, how a bout runs, what scores, what is penalised and when a decision can be reviewed, under World Taekwondo’s rules in force from 1 November 2026, with the AAU Taekwondo differences for youth and Grassroot divisions. Each section links to the exact article and to scenarios you can practise.

A study guide, not a rulebook. It summarises the official rules and doesn’t replace them, and reading or practising here isn’t referee certification. TKDflow isn’t affiliated with World Taekwondo or AAU Taekwondo. References are to WT Competition Rules and Interpretation, in force as of 1 November 2026 (“WT”, printed page numbers) and the AAU Taekwondo Rules Handbook, effective 11-01-2026 (“AAU”).

Who decides what

  • Sensors (PSS) in the trunk and head protectors decide whether a kick lands on a scoring area with a proper level of impact. Those determinations can’t be challenged by video replay. WT Art. 12.2.3, p. 33
  • Judges mark valid points with hand-held scoring devices. They validate punches (with sensing gloves, the PSS detects the impact and the judges confirm the technique) and add the extra points for turning kicks once the PSS has scored the kick. Without PSS, judges score everything. WT Art. 13.1–13.2, p. 35; Art. 20.3.2.2, p. 59
  • The centre referee controls the match, gives every command, and declares penalties, the winner and any retirement. In principle the referee doesn’t award points, but must convene the judges and correct a score when two corner judges ask for it. WT Art. 20.3.2.1, p. 59; Art. 14.1, p. 37
  • The Review Jury reviews instant video replays and tells the referee the decision within 30 seconds; the Technical Assistant watches that scores, penalties and time are shown correctly and keeps a paper record. WT Art. 20.3.2.3–20.3.2.4, p. 59

Practise: a trunk kick the sensors don’t register · a punch the judges validate

How a bout runs

  1. Call: contestants are called three times at the calling desk, starting 30 minutes before the bout; not reporting after the third call is disqualification. WT Art. 10.1, p. 29
  2. Inspection: body, uniform and equipment are inspected at the inspection desk, and the referee may check that the PSS and sensing socks are working (a step that can be skipped to save time). WT Art. 10.2, p. 29; Guideline, p. 30
  3. Entry: the referee calls “Chung, Hong” and the contestants enter with the head protector under the left arm. A contestant not present, or not fully equipped, at that call is treated as withdrawn. WT Art. 10.4.1, p. 29
  4. Bow and start: “Cha-ryeot” (attention), “Kyeong-rye” (bow), head protectors on, then “Joon-bi” (ready) and “Shi-jak” (start). WT Art. 10.4.2–10.4.3, p. 29
  5. Rounds: normally three rounds of two minutes with a one-minute rest; a tie after three rounds goes to a one-minute golden round (not in the best-of-three system). Organisers may set shorter formats. WT Art. 7.1–7.2, p. 25
  6. Each round starts with “Shi-jak” and ends with “Keu-man” (stop), or when the clock expires; a Gam-jeom can still be given after the clock runs out. WT Art. 10.4.4–10.4.5, p. 29
  7. End: “Chung, Hong”, attention and bow again, then the referee raises a hand to the winner’s side: “Chung Seung” (blue wins) or “Hong Seung” (red wins). WT Art. 10.4.7, p. 29

What scores

Permitted: a straight punch with the knuckles of a tightly clenched fist, and kicks with any part of the foot below the ankle bone. Punches and kicks may target the trunk protector (not the spine); only kicks may target the head, above the collarbone. WT Art. 11, p. 32

A back kick counts as a turning kick only if the head and shoulders rotate with it. The turning bonus is cancelled if the PSS didn’t score the kick. WT Art. 12 Explanation #1, p. 34; Art. 13.4, p. 35

Penalties (Gam-jeom)

The referee declares a Gam-jeom for each prohibited act, and it counts as one point for the opponent. WT Art. 14.1–14.3, p. 37 In the last ten seconds of a round, only these give the opponent two points: crossing the boundary line, falling down, and running away or avoiding; a technical review rejected in the last ten seconds also gives two. Every other penalty stays at one. WT Art. 12.3.5.1–12.3.5.2, p. 33

Stoppages, knock-downs and reviews

  • Knock-down: when a legitimate scoring technique puts any part of the body other than the soles on the floor, leaves a contestant staggering without the will or ability to continue, or the referee judges the bout can’t safely continue. WT Art. 17, p. 50
  • The count: “Kal-yeo”, the attacker is sent away, and the referee counts aloud. Counting to eight is compulsory even if the contestant stands up sooner; a contestant who can’t show the will to continue by eight loses by referee stops contest. WT Art. 18.1, p. 51; Explanation #2, p. 53 AAU youth safety divisions have no count for head kicks: see AAU youth.
  • Injury: the referee stops the bout with “Kal-yeo” and gives a minute for first aid by the commission doctor. Medical decisions follow WT Articles 18–19 and the event’s medical staff. This guide gives no medical or return-to-play advice. WT Art. 19.1, p. 55
  • Video replay: a coach has one appeal per bout (blue or red card) for listed decisions: falls, boundary crossings, attacks after “Kal-yeo” or on a fallen opponent, technical points, penalties against their own contestant, uncancelled points after a Gam-jeom, the wrong puncher identified, and head kicks only where no head PSS is used. PSS-scored trunk and head points can’t be appealed. The Review Jury answers within 30 seconds, and its decision is final. In the last five seconds of a round the referee may request a review of possible falls, boundary crossings and attacks after “Kal-yeo” or on a fallen opponent. WT Art. 21.1–21.3, 21.5–21.7, pp. 63–64
  • Technical card: for procedural or administration errors, such as timing, PSS malfunction, points not cancelled after a Gam-jeom, or wrongly registered scores. If the review finds no fault, the requesting coach’s contestant gets a Gam-jeom (two points to the opponent in the last ten seconds). WT Art. 21.4, p. 63; Art. 12.3.5.2, p. 33

For complete procedures, read WT Articles 17–21 (printed pp. 50–64).

AAU youth and Grassroot divisions

AAU Olympic-style sparring uses the same point values: 1, 2, 3, and 4 or 6 for turning kicks. A Gam-jeom gives the opponent one point; in the last ten seconds, two for crossing the line, falling down, running away or avoiding, or a rejected technical card. AAU Art. 9.3, p. 42; Art. 11.2 and 11.4, p. 43 Two divisions change the head rules:

Excessive or hard contactThe referee stops the bout and calls the medic; Gam-jeom for misconduct and the points are cancelledThe referee stops the bout and calls the medic

Two WT rules in this guide don’t carry over to youth safety divisions:

  • No count for a head kick. There is no 8-count for any kick to the head, even a legal one; a head kick strong enough to need an 8-count is excessive contact. Strong kicks to the trunk still follow the standard knock-down procedure. AAU Art. 22.3.7 and 22.3.9, p. 60
  • No fall exception for turning head kicks. A contestant who falls after a turning kick to the head gets a Gam-jeom for falling, and turning kicks to the head aren’t considered when deciding superiority or penalties for falling. AAU Art. 22.4.3–22.4.4, p. 60

Both articles also set out medical and return-to-play rules after head contact; follow the official text and the event’s medical staff. AAU Art. 22.6–22.7, p. 60; Art. 23.3, p. 61

Practise: Grassroot head touch · Grassroot miss · youth spinning head kick

Becoming a referee

Referees qualify through their organisation’s courses, seminars and supervised events; at World Taekwondo events they must hold an International Referee Certificate registered by World Taekwondo. WT Art. 20.3.1, p. 59
